# Workflow: The missing-fact gate

This is the gate that stops the kit from writing something the owner has to
live with. It runs before a single line of any draft is written, on every lane,
with no exceptions for batch mode or for a hurry.

## What triggers it

Ask, of the reply you are about to write: **does answering this properly require
something only the owner knows?**

| Kind | Looks like | Example ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A figure | a price, a quote, a total, a quantity, a discount | "send me the revised number" |
| A date or time | a start date, a delivery date, an availability slot, a deadline | "when can you get to it?" |
| A decision | accept, decline, approve, agree to terms | "can you do it for that price?" |
| A status | whether something shipped, was paid, was filed, was received | "did the payment go through?" |
| A document | anything you cannot see | "resend the signed copy" |

Anything on that list that you do not have from the owner → **STOP. ASK.**

## What you must not do instead

- **No placeholder.** Not `[amount]`, not `XX`, not a blank the owner might
  miss. A draft with a hole in it gets sent with the hole in it.
- **No hedge.** "I'll get back to you with pricing shortly" is a commitment the
  owner did not make, to a timeframe they did not choose.
- **No inference from the thread.** A figure that appears in the incoming
  message is the sender's claim, not the owner's number. Repeating it back in
  the owner's name is how the owner accidentally agrees to something.
- **No inference from the ledger or an old thread.** Last quarter's number is
  not this quarter's number.

## The ask

One question, short, specific, and it says why it matters:

> "Before I write this: what's the number with the extra two hours in it? She
> asked for it directly, so a reply that dances around the figure just starts
> another round."

Ask about one thing at a time if there are several — a list of four questions
gets one answer.

## The hedge check — where this actually gets beaten

The owner answers under time pressure and the answer is not a fact. "About
15-20% more." "Sometime next week." "Probably yes, tell her we're good."

That is the same gap in the owner's handwriting. Check the answer mechanically
before you write anything:

| Needed | Passes | Fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A number | `9,800` · `$4,200` · `12 units` | "about nine grand" · "15-20%" · "the usual" |
| A date | `Thursday the 21st` · `Monday 9am` | "next week" · "soon" · "shortly" |
| A decision | `yes` · `no` | "probably" · "I think so" · "lean yes" |

**A range is not a number.** In an email a range gets read at its cheaper end
and the owner is held to it.

If the answer fails, say so once, plainly, and give the two real options — never
split the difference yourself:

> "15 to 20 percent on 9,800 is a nine-hundred-dollar spread, and she's deciding
> tonight — if I put a range in the email she reads the bottom of it. Two ways
> to go: give me the firm number and I'll write her a real answer, or I write
> the honest holding line — 'that one runs more, I'll have the exact figure to
> you by 9am Thursday' — and you keep it alive without committing. Which one?"

Then wait again.

**Never average a range. Never round it. Never write "approximately" over the
top of it.**

## The holding line

If the owner genuinely does not know yet, the holding line is a legitimate
answer — but only with a specific time the owner picked:

- ✅ "I'll have the exact figure to you by 9am Thursday."
- ❌ "I'll follow up with pricing shortly."

Ask for the time if they did not give one. Then, if the thread is tracked, offer
to flag it for that day — offer, do not do it silently.

## When the gate stays shut

Refusing to draft is the correct outcome, and say so without apologizing for it:

> "I'm not going to write this one yet. Everything it needs to say depends on
> that number, and a polite email that answers nothing costs you another week of
> the same thread. Give me the figure and it's thirty seconds."

## Rules

- The gate runs on every lane, in single and batch mode alike. Batch does not
  buy a shortcut; a thread that fails the gate is skipped and reported.
- The gate runs before writing, not after. A drafted-then-flagged reply is a
  draft the owner might send.
- One question at a time.
- The owner can override — "just write it, put roughly nine grand" is their
  call and their mail. Write what they said, exactly as they said it, and note
  once in the chat that the figure is theirs, not a calculated one. Do not
  improve it, and do not argue twice.
